## What was run, and why it was run this way

PLAN-002 section 20 requires that the first implementation-generated **Layer A source-aligned
overlay** be presented at a Visual/Geometry evidence gate (NA-4), and the roadmap's NA-5 is a
smoke run of the public-domain sample. Rather than produce two unrelated artifacts, both were
generated from **one geometry**:

1. The wall centrelines, room polygons and openings were **measured** from
   `samples/Sample_Floorplan.jpg` with Pillow and NumPy — not estimated by eye. Scripts are in
   the session scratchpad and are not committed; their results are reproduced here and in the
   annotation.
2. That geometry was emitted twice: as a **Layer A DXF in millimetres** (case A, NA-4) and as a
   **hand-authored `floorplan_annotation` 1.0.0 document** over the original JPEG (case B, NA-5).
3. Both were ingested with `ingest_project` and parsed with the real `parse_run`.

The point of the shared geometry is that PLAN-002 section 6 claims the annotation adapter "mirrors
the same primitive semantics as DXF so geometry projection can be compared." Nobody had tested
that claim. This does.

### The one-quantum discrepancy, and what it turned out to be

The first run produced **different** projection hashes. The difference was exactly one quantum
(0.0001 m) on exactly one window: 0.7001 m on the DXF path against 0.7000 m on the annotation
path, with its centre 0.1 mm apart. Walls and room polygons were identical.

The cause was in the **fixture generator, not the parser**: opening endpoints were computed as
`half_px = width_m / scale / 2` and then converted pixels → millimetres, and that float round
trip loses a tenth of a millimetre. Placing the endpoints directly in millimetres removed it and
the hashes became identical. Recorded because a reader who sees only the final "identical" line
should know the first attempt disagreed and why — the parser was never at fault, and the check was
sensitive enough to catch 0.1 mm.

## Visual gate assessment (NA-4)

Both overlays were served over localhost and rendered in a real browser, then inspected and
zoomed. Rendered captures are committed beside the SVGs:

- `evidence/PLAN-002/visual-gate/na4-layer-a-dxf-overlay.svg` and `...-rendered.jpg`
- `evidence/PLAN-002/visual-gate/na5-sample-raster-overlay.svg` and `...-rendered.jpg`

What is right:

- **Source alignment is exact.** In the raster overlay every drawn primitive sits on the wall it
  describes; zoomed inspection of the kitchen, the entrance and the north wall shows no drift.
- The source raster is **embedded** as a data URI, so the overlay is self-contained, with no
  external reference to resolve — which is what the mandatory-overlay clause asks for.
- Openings are marked at the measured gaps, doors and windows distinguishable.
- The DXF overlay renders the same building in vector form with no raster, as expected for that
  adapter, and its outline is recognisably the same plan.

What is wrong, stated rather than glossed:

- **The labels clutter the picture.** Every entity's opaque id (`w-7dc001ec1f6e`,
  `r-cfa2ea3af0eb`) is drawn as text, and at this entity count they overlap each other and the
  source. The overlay is correct and hard to read. That is a usability defect in an artifact whose
  entire purpose is human judgement, and it is a direct consequence of GC3-6 replacing source
  names with opaque tokens — the right security decision with an unpriced legibility cost. It is
  not a correctness or security defect, and no acceptance criterion currently forbids it.

**Gate disposition.** Accepted on delegated authority as satisfying the overlay's content
requirements, with the label-clutter defect recorded. Stated plainly: **no human eye has seen
these artifacts.** Section 20 makes this Moshe's gate, and the delegation of 2026-08-10 cannot
substitute for a visual judgement — it can only carry the work forward until he looks. He can
overturn this on sight, and the label clutter is the most likely reason he would.

## Declared simplifications in the annotation

Not defects; choices, recorded so nobody mistakes the annotation for a complete digitisation.

- **S1** The north-west angled bay (the octagonal breakfast area) is replaced by a straight north
  wall from x=101 to x=280.5 at y=127.5. The bay is three short diagonals plus two windows and
  adds nothing to a pipeline smoke test. The kitchen therefore loses the bay's floor area.
- **S2** Six openings are declared — two doors measured as real gaps in the wall lines, four
  windows measured from their symbols. The plan carries roughly twenty.
- **S3** Room polygons follow wall centrelines, so each room's area is its centreline area,
  slightly larger than its clear internal area.

## Scale, and why it is defensible rather than assumed

`scale_m_per_px = 0.0213`, from four independent measurements spanning 0.0208–0.0219: two interior
door gaps (37 px and 39 px against a 0.81 m leaf) and two wall bands (an 11 px exterior against
24 cm, a 6 px partition against 12.5 cm). The drawing carries no overall dimension line, so no
better anchor exists in it.

The rejected alternative is recorded: if the background dotted grid is a 50 cm construction grid
the scale would be 0.025 m/px, 17% away. It was rejected because the grid's real pitch is an
assumption about an unknown CAD template while wall thicknesses and door leaves are physical
conventions — but if a later measurement contradicts 0.0213, that is the first alternative to try.

Note the plan's mixed provenance: American room names ("Eat-in Kitchen", "1/2 Bath", "Family")
with German CAD annotations (`BRH 87.50` is sill height in cm; `16 Stg 17.5/22.7` is 16 risers at
17.5/22.7 cm).

## What is NOT claimed

- **This is not accuracy evidence.** PLAN-002 section 13 forbids an accuracy claim without labels,
  and this image has no labelled ground truth. The scale is defensible, not measured from the
  building. Room areas are plausible, not correct.
- **G1 is not claimed.** The gate needs the NA-3f code review to return and, per section 20,
  Moshe's own look at the overlay.
- The `style_reference` in both runs is a **generated neutral placeholder**, because the sample
  ships without one and the parse stage never reads it. It is not passed off as a real style input.
- No H200, GPU, cloud, remote execution or spending occurred. G7/G8 remain deferred to Part 2.
